# Cadence by duration

`scripts/plan.py` prints the dated version. This file says what changes between lengths.

| | 1 week | 2 weeks | 1 month |
|---|---|---|---|
| Best for | One product at a deal price, a flash offer | A launch, a two-phase sale (early access, then everyone) | A season, a subscription push, an evergreen offer |
| Emails | 3: opening, mid-week, last day | 5: opening, proof, new angle, 3 days left, last day | 8: one or two a week, plus opening and closing |
| Texts | 2: open, ends tonight | 2 to 3 | 3 to 4, never more than one a week outside open/close |
| Ad creative | One batch, no refresh | One refresh at day 7 | Refresh weekly |
| Ad learning | Start ads 2 to 3 days before the offer if the copy allows | Same | Not needed |
| Reviews | Day 3 and day 5 | Twice a week | Weekly, plus day 3 |
| Urgency | Real end date, said once a day at most | Phase change at the midpoint gives a second "opening" | Use monthly themes, not a countdown |

## Rules for every length

- One offer in every message. If the offer has tiers, show the same tiers everywhere.
- The end date is real. No extension emails.
- Rest the list: after a campaign, at least 3 days with no promotional sends.
- Back-to-back deal weeks train customers to wait for the deal price. For brands with a subscription, lead with the subscription and run a true sale at most once a month.
- Two-phase campaigns: if the store can't limit an automatic discount to one group, announce early access to the list first and to everyone later, rather than building a code.

## Launch-day order

1. Discounts live and tested in a cart.
2. Theme published; note the theme to roll back to.
3. Ads flipped; check effective status after 10 minutes.
4. First email and push scheduled.
5. Organic posts pushed.

## Close-out

Discounts and ad sets should end on their own schedule. Verify they did, restore the theme, then report: orders, net sales, ad spend, new customers, new subscribers, cost per new customer against the cap.
